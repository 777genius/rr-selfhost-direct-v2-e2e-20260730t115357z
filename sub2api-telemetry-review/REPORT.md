The supplied official Linux v6.8 `include/linux/mm.h` defines `get_mm_rss` as the sum of file, anonymous and shmem counters, and `get_mm_hiwater_rss(mm)` as `max(mm->hiwater_rss, get_mm_rss(mm))`. `update_hiwater_rss` is a separate helper; reading this expression does not itself store the currently returned maximum into `hiwater_rss`. With a stored watermark below a temporarily higher current counter, a later lower current counter can yield a lower returned value without a reset. A possible stronger physical bound needs more than a cgroup peak. RSS counts each resident mapping, whereas memcg normally charges a physical page once to an owning cgroup. File pages can already be charged elsewhere (including private file mappings before COW); shared anonymous/shmem pages and alias mappings introduce further differences. A cgroup peak alone can therefore be smaller than process RSS. This snapshot demonstrates that: the first ready sample has VmRSS 76713984 and cgroup peak 24952832 bytes. That is not itself invalid evidence.

At a particular coherent map state, the sum of all mapped virtual extents is a conservative upper bound on resident mapped bytes, including aliases. To extend it through the whole interval one must prove an all-time bound on those extents, not take the largest observed map sample. A tighter conditional construction is `C_peak + E_max`, where E covers every RSS mapping contribution not represented once in the cgroup charge: externally charged file/shmem/shared pages, duplicate aliases, and unsupported/bypass memory types. It requires a kernel/topology audit, complete mapping classification, charge ownership or conservative full extents, alias multiplicity, and a proven maximum valid throughout the interval. The cgroup ceiling alone and a single `/proc/maps` inventory do not provide that proof. Even identical snapshots can hide a transient mmap/unmap between them. The supplied primary excerpts do not establish all those premises. Therefore neither this formula nor `cgroup peak + sampled file extent` is emitted as a proven bound. The collector records all virtual extents and a deliberately broad external-candidate extent (inode-backed, shared, or named mappings), both explicitly observational. That candidate sum is not a complete ownership proof. Actual smaps and continuously justified mapping bounds could strengthen a future disposable rerun; until then physical peak/delta stay NOT RUN.
